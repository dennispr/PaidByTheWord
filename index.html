Naive writer! Neither did we!

Nobody watches movies for the new stuff. They want the same characters, the same locations, the same everything — just enough plot to justify a ticket price.

Now get in there and write us a sequel.

How to Play

You're a broke writer hired to pitch a sequel. The studio doesn't want originality — they want to recycle what worked the first time. Your pay is tied to the original script: the more often a word appeared there, the more they'll pay you for it.

How it works:

  • Write 5 pitches — sentences describing your sequel idea
  • Green underline = high-value word (appears often in the original)
  • Gray = in the movie, but low frequency
  • Strikethrough = not in the original at all — too creative!
  • One pitch must earn $150 to cover rent — pitches don't add together

Common words like "the" and "and" don't score — focus on names, places, and story-specific words.
